\footnotesize
\textbf{The headline of this table is the gap between the two blocks.} Our camera-frame error
(C-MPJPE$_{\text{abs}}$ 35.2) is essentially unchanged by the oracle (33.0), and our 30-frame
window-aligned error is already 35.0\,mm, yet global W-MPJPE is 200.9. Handing the \emph{same}
per-frame hand predictions a ground-truth camera trajectory collapses W-MPJPE to 61.5 on the
identical 157 sequences. Long-window global error is therefore dominated by camera-trajectory
drift, not by hand pose: every method in the upper block sits within 38\% of every other
(200.9--276.6), a spread far smaller than the 3.3$\times$ drop the oracle delivers. This is why we
report W-MPJPE alongside window-aligned metrics rather than instead of them.

$^{a}$ Ours + DROID-SLAM: our per-frame camera-space hands composed with the same offline DROID-SLAM
trajectory used by the baseline rows. It does \emph{not} beat our own online predicted extrinsics
(201.7 vs 200.9), i.e. replacing our camera head with offline SLAM buys nothing at this window length.
$^{b}$ HaWoR: full pipeline (DROID-SLAM + Metric3D + motion + infiller), run end-to-end, scored
identically. $^{c}$ WiLoR, HaMeR and HaPTIC do no camera tracking, so each is composed with the
\emph{same} DROID-SLAM trajectory and scored identically. None produces metric depth, so their
absolute camera-frame column reflects a fitted rather than a predicted scale and should not be read
as a metric-depth result; their root-relative and window-aligned columns are the meaningful ones.
$^{d}$ Dyn-HaMR is a per-sequence test-time optimization method. In the input-matched setup its own
metric-depth stage is bypassed, so world scale is unconstrained and absolute placement diverges
(C-MPJPE$_{\text{abs}}$ 1327); only its scale-invariant columns are meaningful. 156 of 157 sequences.
$^{e}$ Reported over the 60-sequence subset for which a ground-truth camera track was available at
the time of the run (120 segments), so it is \emph{not} directly comparable cell-for-cell with the
157-sequence rows above. The full-157 equivalent is the last oracle row (61.5).
$^{f}$ Trajectory-error decomposition on all 157 sequences: our own chained trajectory with the
rotation, the translation, or both replaced by ground truth, hand predictions held fixed. Because
only the camera changes, the camera-frame column is unchanged by construction (35.2); root-relative
is omitted for the same reason. Translation is the larger of the two error sources
(123.9 vs 166.2), and neither alone recovers the joint oracle (61.5).
