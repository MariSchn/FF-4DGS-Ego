## 1. The protocol, as locked

| | |
|---|---|
| Clip length | **T = 32**, with the frame count drawn per batch as `n ~ U{2..32}` at random sorted indices |
| Windows reported | short **30** frames, long **100** frames (Hand3R-matched, previously 128) |
| Training stores | HOT3D, OakInk2, ARCTIC, DexYCB, **+ TACO** (replacing Re:InterHand) |
| Evaluation stores | **H2O and HOI4D, both fully held out** |
| Boxes | our detector, detbox v3, for every learned row |

**Pool change, 2026-08-05.** Re:InterHand is **out**. Its ego cameras are re-randomised every
frame: focal jumps 166 px between consecutive frames (spread 369-917 within one capture) and the
camera centre moves 755 mm, which is ~11 m/s of "head" motion. `envmap_per_frame` is the only ego
variant Meta ships, so there is no coherent-trajectory version to switch to. Its per-frame labels
are sound (99.8% of in-image MANO joints land on the shipped hand mask under the measured
`w2c_direct` + fisheye conventions) but it is a per-frame relighting set, not egocentric video, and
our method is a video method.

**TACO** (CVPR 2024) replaces it: 2,317 bimanual ego RGB-D sequences with per-frame world-to-camera
extrinsics, *constant* per-sequence intrinsics, no lens distortion, and full MANO in the same
`(48,)` axis-angle no-PCA convention our converters already parse. Verified from its loader code
rather than its abstract, which is the mistake that let Re:InterHand in.

Consequence that has not yet been absorbed: **every number currently in the paper was produced by
a 16-frame model trained on HOI4D-367**, so it is in-domain rather than zero-shot and provisional
until the retrain lands. The paper says so in a todo rather than pretending otherwise.

## 2. What has been established

### Results that survived
| finding | number | status |
|---|---|---|
| Depth-transfer law | `C_abs ~= 60 + 0.5 x abs(depth shift)`, r = 0.76 over 148 seqs | holds; registered prediction 64.6 landed at 66.2 |
| Camera dominates long-window world error | W 200.9 -> **61.5** with a GT camera track, camera-frame unchanged | strongest analysis we have |

### Results that refuted our own claims
| claim | verdict |
|---|---|
| Reconstruction features are the advantage | **null** - a frozen DINOv2 matches or beats them |
| Box geometry is the depth cue | **refuted** - destroying it costs 0.7 mm, inside the +/-0.8 mm seed band |
| `kp3d_abs` specifically is necessary | **refuted** - the translation term alone reaches 23.0 against 22.8 |
| Our recipe matches a tuned per-frame baseline | **refuted** - fine-tuned HaMeR reaches 23.4 against our 35.6 on identical boxes |

## 8. The open question that matters most

Our ablations have now examined the frozen backbone, the box-geometry depth cue, and the absolute
losses. **None of the three is where our accuracy comes from.** The two components we actually
added, feature-space cropping and local-global feature fusion, are the only ones never isolated.

Four runs at matched budget (pixel crop vs feature crop, local-only vs with fusion) either hands
the paper its missing mechanism or establishes that the contribution is the recipe and the
analysis, in which case the depth-transfer law should become the headline. This is the highest-value
experiment available and it is not blocked by anything.

## 9. Infrastructure lessons worth keeping

- **Both inodes and bytes bind, at different times.** On 2026-08-04 it was inodes: Re:InterHand's
  one-JSON-per-frame-per-hand layout consumed the entire 1.5M file allocation and killed every
  running job while only 1.55 of 2.5 TB was used. By 2026-08-05 inodes were fine and **bytes** were
  the wall. Guard against whichever is closer, and always against the HARD limit, not the soft one:
  exceeding soft starts a grace timer, exceeding hard write-locks the filesystem instantly for every
  running job.
- **Cache size is a coverage decision, so make it explicitly.** HOT3D at clip_stride 16 enumerates
  29,819 clips = 933 GB, more than ARCTIC and OakInk2 combined, and it had reached only 67 of 136
  sequences before being cancelled - it was buying 229 near-duplicate windows into half the scenes
  instead of covering all of them. Rebuilt at stride 48 it is ~10,000 clips over every sequence.
  Any build stride that is a **multiple of the training `clip_stride`** is safe, because training
  enumerates `range(0, N-32+1, 16)` and then *filters* to clips present in the cache rather than
  failing on a miss (`train_hand_head.py:494`, `:545`).
- **A guard that blocks safe work gets switched off.** The first prune guard refused because a job
  named `varlen` was running, when that job read only the arctic and oakink2 caches. It now asks
  which configs actually reference the target directory.
- **Never extract DexYCB whole.** Over a million small files. Convert per subject, then delete;
  our store format collapses a sequence to 8 files.
- **Cache-building and training have different memory profiles.** Building a 32-frame cache runs
  the full 1.1B backbone and OOMed at batch 2 on 24 GB. Training from cache skips the backbone
  entirely and needs 128 MB per batch.
- **A 16-frame cache cannot be spliced into a 32-frame one.** The backbone attends globally across
  the clip, so tokens from a 16-frame pass have never seen the other half.
- **Validate-only modes must not share a success counter.** `--validate` printed `wrote=1` after
  writing nothing.
- **Vet a dataset from its loader code, never from its abstract.** Re:InterHand's page and paper
  both read as an egocentric hand dataset; only the per-frame camera files showed the camera is
  re-sampled every frame. A directory of numbered images is not evidence of video. The cheap test is
  now standard: consecutive-frame deltas of camera translation and focal length, before conversion.
- **Prefer measuring a convention to inferring it.** Re:InterHand's distortion model and extrinsic
  direction were settled in one run by projecting MANO joints under all eight candidate combinations
  and scoring against the shipped hand masks (80.9% for the winner, 2.9% for the runner-up family).
  The magnitude argument pointed the same way, but the measurement is what we can defend.
