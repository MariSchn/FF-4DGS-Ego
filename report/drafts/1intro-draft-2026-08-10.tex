\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Egocentric video is the natural input for understanding manipulation: the camera sits where the
actor looks, and the hands are where intention meets the world. Using that signal downstream, for
imitation learning, for AR interaction, or for reasoning about what a person did, requires knowing
where the hands are in \emph{metric} terms, in a coordinate frame attached to the scene rather than
to a crop around the wrist.

Current hand estimators do not provide this. State-of-the-art single-image methods
\citep{pavlakos2024hamer, potamias2024wilor} crop tightly around a detected hand and regress pose
under a weak-perspective camera. The crop is what makes them accurate, because it removes nuisance
variation and lets a large model specialise. It is also what makes them non-metric: hand size and
hand distance trade off against each other at nearly constant reprojection, so the crop alone cannot
determine how far away the hand is. Our own measurements show the split clearly. On HOI4D, WiLoR and
HaMeR reach root-relative errors of $27.2$ and $30.3$\,mm while their absolute errors are $83.4$ and
$88.0$\,mm. These methods have an articulation model and no placement model.

The usual repair composes such an estimator with a separate pipeline that supplies the missing
geometry, a SLAM trajectory or a monocular depth predictor or both
\citep{zhang2025hawor, dynhamr}. This works, and it is what the strongest published baselines do.
It also inherits the failure modes of every stage, needs the whole sequence before it can produce
anything, and leaves the hand and the scene in coordinate systems that must be reconciled afterwards.

We take the opposite route and ask what a hand branch attached to a \emph{frozen} feed-forward
reconstruction backbone recovers in a single pass. The backbone already computes, for its own
purposes, the evidence a crop discards: the surfaces the hand rests on, reaches toward and occludes.
Our contribution turns out to be less an architecture for consuming that evidence than an account of
\emph{what makes egocentric hand estimation metric}, and two of our three findings are negative or
data-side rather than architectural. We report them as we measured them.

\paragraph{Absolute supervision, not architecture, is what buys metric placement.}
A regressor trained against root-relative targets is never asked how far away the hand is, so it
never learns. Supervising the decoder's translation output directly against absolute camera-frame
joints fixes this and adds no parameters. Crossing the two absolute terms in a $2\times2$ shows the
effect is causal and coarse: with neither term the model collapses to a near-constant depth at
$682.8$\,mm, while either term alone recovers almost everything, $27.1$ or $23.0$ against $22.8$ for
the pair. The particular loss is interchangeable. Its presence is not.

\paragraph{Cross-dataset transfer is governed by depth coverage, not data volume.}
Absolute placement is partly prior-driven, so a model inherits the depth distribution of its
training mixture. We find that error on an unseen dataset grows with how far its hands sit from that
distribution, and that what matters is whether the test store lies inside the mixture's
\emph{support}. Trained on a single dataset our model reaches $184.8$\,mm zero-shot on H2O; trained
on a mixture whose depth support contains H2O it reaches $\mathbf{66.2}$, a $64\%$ reduction on an
identical split, at an in-domain cost of $6.9$\,mm. We registered this prediction before the run.

\paragraph{The scene branch is a conditioning signal, and we report where it does not help.}
Our hand branch crops in feature space rather than pixel space, so both hands reuse the single
backbone pass the scene already required, and the crop tokens attend to the full frame. A controlled
substitution over three seeds then finds that a frozen generic DINOv2 backbone is at least as good as
the reconstruction backbone, $22.2 \pm 0.7$ against $23.6 \pm 0.8$, while a randomly initialised
frozen backbone is clearly worse at $27.7$. Pretrained features are necessary; reconstruction
features are not, so we do not attribute our results to them. A second control degrades the detection box at evaluation time and finds that removing
its per-frame geometry entirely costs $0.7$\,mm, inside our seed band, refuting our own hypothesis
that box size was acting as a depth cue.

We evaluate against HaWoR, HaMeR, WiLoR, HaPTIC and Dyn-HaMR, running each ourselves on our own
detector boxes rather than reprinting published numbers, and we state the comparison that goes
against us: applying our own absolute-supervision recipe as a full fine-tune of HaMeR and scoring it
on identical boxes reaches $23.4$\,mm against our $35.8$. Our contribution is therefore not accuracy.
It is that a $46.3$\,M-parameter branch on a frozen backbone recovers metric hands in one
feed-forward pass, with no SLAM, no monocular depth stage and no test-time optimisation, together
with an account of which ingredients that depends on.
