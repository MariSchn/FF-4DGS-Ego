\begin{abstract}
Egocentric hand estimators are accurate about articulation and silent about placement. Cropping
around a detected hand is what makes them work and also what makes them non-metric, since hand size
and hand distance trade off at nearly constant reprojection. On HOI4D, WiLoR and HaMeR reach
root-relative errors of $27.2$ and $30.3$\,mm while their absolute errors are $83.4$ and $88.0$\,mm.
The usual repair bolts on SLAM or monocular depth, which recovers scale at the cost of a multi-stage
pipeline that needs the whole sequence. We instead attach a hand branch to a \emph{frozen}
feed-forward reconstruction backbone, crop in feature space so both hands reuse the single pass the
scene already required, and ask what that recovers in one shot. The answer is mostly not
architectural, and we report it as we measured it. First, absolute supervision is causally necessary
and coarse: removing it collapses the model to a near-constant depth at $682.8$\,mm, while either of
two absolute terms alone recovers almost everything. Second, cross-dataset transfer is governed by
the \emph{depth coverage} of the training mixture rather than its size; a mixture whose support
contains the test store cuts zero-shot error on H2O from $184.8$ to $\mathbf{66.2}$\,mm, a $64\%$
reduction, for $6.9$\,mm in domain. Third, two controls refute our own hypotheses: a generic
self-supervised backbone matches the reconstruction backbone, and removing the detection box's
per-frame geometry costs $0.7$\,mm, inside our seed band. We run HaWoR, HaMeR, WiLoR, HaPTIC and
Dyn-HaMR ourselves on shared detector boxes, and state the comparison that goes against us: our own
recipe applied as a full fine-tune of HaMeR reaches $23.4$\,mm on identical boxes against our
$35.8$. What we offer is not accuracy but a trade, one feed-forward pass and $46.3$\,M trained
parameters with no SLAM and no test-time optimisation, and an account of which ingredients metric
egocentric hand estimation actually depends on.
\end{abstract}
